# S corporation ordinary business loss on Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) in United States

This Guide covers how an S corporation completes Form 1120-S and Schedule K-1 to pass through a box 1 ordinary business loss, and how the individual shareholder determines how much of that loss is actually deductible once the basis, at-risk and passive activity rules are applied. It is for S corporations that already have a valid election in effect and for the shareholders who receive a Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) showing a loss in box 1. Figures are for tax year 2025, which is the most recent tax year for which the IRS had published the Form 1120-S instructions, the Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) shareholder instructions and the Form 7203 instructions at the time this Guide was drafted; the IRS had not yet issued 2026 versions of these instructions.

## Who this is for


Don't file Form 1120-S unless the corporation has filed or is attaching Form 2553, Election by a Small Business Corporation.
 This Guide is for such an electing S corporation and for its individual shareholders who receive a Schedule K-1 (Form 1120-S) showing an amount in box 1, described on the form as 
"Ordinary business income (loss)."
 It does not cover partnerships filing Form 1065, C corporations filing Form 1120, or the rental real estate boxes (box 2 and box 3) of the Schedule K-1, which carry separate rules not addressed here.

## The method, step by step

1. Confirm the S corporation election is in place: the corporation must have filed or be attaching Form 2553 before Form 1120-S is filed, per the instructions in the table above (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s).
2. Keep the corporation's supporting records for the retention period in the table above, and keep the corporation's property basis records for as long as needed to figure basis (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s).
3. Prepare Form 1120-S, Schedule K, and each shareholder's Schedule K-1, entering the shareholder's pro rata share of ordinary business income (loss) in box 1 of Schedule K-1 (https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1120ssk.pdf).
4. File Form 1120-S by the due date in the table above, and pay any tax due by that same date, not including extensions (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120s).
5. The shareholder computes stock basis and, separately, debt basis for any loans made to the corporation, generally using Form 7203, before claiming any box 1 loss (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-stock-and-debt-basis).
6. The shareholder applies the loss limitations in the order set out in the table above: basis (Form 7203), then at-risk (Form 6198), then passive activity (Form 8582), then excess business loss (Form 461) (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i7203).
7. Any loss disallowed for lack of basis is suspended and treated as incurred by the corporation in the following tax year with respect to that shareholder (https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporation-stock-and-debt-basis).
8. Once the limitations have been applied, a shareholder who materially participated reports the allowed box 1 amount on Schedule E (Form 1040), line 28, column (i) or (k), following the instructions in the table above (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i1120ssk).

## Ask the client first

- Did the shareholder materially participate in the S corporation's trade or business this year, or is the activity passive to them?
- What was the shareholder's stock basis, and any outstanding loan (debt) basis, at the start of the year, before this year's loss?
- Did the shareholder receive any non-dividend distributions from the corporation this year that would reduce stock basis before the loss is applied?
- Are there prior-year losses suspended under the basis rules that must be combined with this year's loss on Form 7203?
- Is the shareholder "at risk" for the full amount allocated, or are there nonrecourse amounts or guarantees that could trigger Form 6198?
- Has Form 2553 actually been filed and accepted for the corporation, and is its tax year confirmed as calendar or fiscal?

## When to refuse or refer

- Partnership Schedule K-1 (Form 1065) losses are not covered by this Guide; partnership basis and at-risk rules differ from the S corporation rules described here.
- C corporation returns (Form 1120) are out of scope; the pass-through loss rules in this Guide apply only to electing S corporations.
- Rental real estate loss reporting (Schedule K-1 boxes 2 and 3) is not covered here and uses separate passive activity rules from box 1.
- The excess business loss limitation (Form 461) is named in the order of limitations above but its mechanics are not worked through in this Guide.
- Any dispute over whether the corporation's S election is valid, terminated, or was ever properly filed should be referred, since Form 1120-S should not be filed without a filed or attached Form 2553.
- State-level treatment of an S corporation ordinary business loss is not covered by this Guide, which addresses only the federal Form 1120-S and Schedule K-1 rules.
